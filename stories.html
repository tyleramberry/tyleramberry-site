Write the build you're proudest of.

Aim for 900–1,400 words, first person, plain language. Answer these in order and it'll mostly write itself:

  1. What was the situation, and why did it matter to someone?
  2. What did everyone assume, and what did you see differently?
  3. The hardest decision: what were the options, and why did you pick yours?
  4. What did you actually ship, and how do you know it worked?
  5. What would you tell yourself on day one?

The miss I learned the most from. Written soon.

[PLACEHOLDER: one-sentence dek. What you got wrong, and what it cost.]

Draft slot · for Tyler

Write the one you'd take back.

The honest version reads stronger than the polished one. Same length as the success story:

  1. What were you trying to do, and what did you believe going in?
  2. Where was the first sign it was going wrong, and why did you miss or ignore it?
  3. What did it cost: time, money, a customer, trust?
  4. What do you do differently now? Name the rule, process, or habit.
  5. Where has that rule already paid off?
