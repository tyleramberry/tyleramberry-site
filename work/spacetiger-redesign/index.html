    One request.
    Five steps. No guessing.

    Here’s what happens when a customer email lands in a system we’ve built. It reads, it looks things up in your records, and it waits for you where it should.

    01 · SIGNAL

    It arrives

    A customer email lands in the shared inbox. No one has to forward it anywhere.

    02 · UNDERSTAND

    It reads

    Tig works out what’s being asked, who’s asking, and how urgent it is.

    It checks

    It finds the answer in your documents and history, and names its sources. If the answer isn’t there, it says so.

    04 · CHECKPOINT

    You approve

    Anything consequential waits for a person. You decide what runs on its own.

    05 · ACT

    It’s done

    How we build

    Careful by default.
    Useful from day one.

    People trust AI systems that behave predictably. Every system we ship follows these six rules. We write them into the spec and into the code.

    RULE_01 ● ENFORCED

    Spec before build

    We write down what the system will and won’t do, and you sign off on it before any building starts.

    RULE_02 ● ENFORCED

    You approve what matters

    A message to a customer or a change to a record can wait for a person to approve it. You decide what runs on its own.

    RULE_03 ● ENFORCED

    It stops rather than guesses

    If the answer isn’t in your information, the system flags it and doesn’t make something up.

    RULE_04 ● ENFORCED

    Least access needed

    Read-only access wherever the job allows, and your data stays in accounts you control.

    RULE_05 ● ENFORCED

    Sensitive files stay out

    Files an AI should never read, like credential sheets, are blocked in code, not left to good habits.

    RULE_06 ● ENFORCED

    Honest about the options

    If an off-the-shelf tool or a better process is the right answer, we’ll say so before you spend a dollar on a build.
